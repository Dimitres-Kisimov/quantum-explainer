# Why an honest quantum explainer is worth building — an applicability case

*This repo is an educational tool, not a product, so framing it in euros would
be dishonest. Instead: here is the problem it addresses, what it verifiably
does, who benefits, and what I am explicitly **not** claiming.*

## The situation

Public understanding of quantum computing sits between two bad extremes:
textbook walls of matrices that only specialists can read, and marketing copy
about machines that "try every answer at once" — a claim that is simply false
[Bennett–Bernstein–Brassard–Vazirani 1997; Preskill 2018]. Students meeting
the subject for the first time, and professionals who need to judge quantum
claims in their own field, both lack a place to build *correct* intuition:
what superposition, interference, and entanglement actually do, and — just as
important — what today's noisy, small machines cannot do.

## What the app does about it

- **A verified simulator, not slideware.** Every state the app shows comes
  from a hand-written 1–2 qubit state-vector simulator (`sim.js`, zero
  dependencies) covered by **107 physics/behaviour assertions** in plain Node:
  H|0⟩ gives 50/50, H·H interference returns |0⟩, the Bell state measures
  {00: 0.5, 11: 0.5} with a failing factorability check, RY(π) ≈ X up to
  global phase, norms stay 1 to 1e-10 through long circuits, and Deutsch's
  algorithm returns the right constant/balanced verdict from a single query.
- **Visual intuition with the numbers attached.** A draggable Bloch sphere
  (reduced states in two-qubit mode, so entanglement is honestly drawn as
  both arrows collapsing to the centre), phase dials for every complex
  amplitude, animated gate sweeps along each gate's true rotation axis, and a
  step scrubber to replay any intermediate state.
- **Cited lessons, including the anti-hype one.** Learn mode covers
  superposition, measurement, interference, and entanglement — plus a
  dedicated **"What quantum computers are NOT"** section (parallel-worlds
  myth, NISQ limits, decoherence, error-correction overhead, why RSA is not
  being broken today). Every claim is demonstrated live on the simulator or
  cited to one of eight references (Nielsen & Chuang; Preskill; Shor; Grover;
  Gidney & Ekerå; BBBV; IBM Quantum Learning; Deutsch & Jozsa).

## Who benefits

- **Students** meeting qubits for the first time: they can *poke* the
  formalism at the only scale where it can be shown completely, instead of
  memorizing it.
- **Educators and explainers**: an installable, offline classroom demo with
  reproducible (seeded) measurement runs — no accounts, no network, no setup
  beyond opening a page.
- **The portfolio itself**: in a field saturated with overclaiming, an
  artifact whose central design decision is *refusing to hype* — and which
  backs that stance with automated checks — is a credibility signal about
  how I communicate technical subjects.

## Measured quality evidence

- **107 simulator assertions** pass in plain Node (`node test/sim.test.mjs`).
- **79 structural checks** pass (`node tools/verify.mjs`): manifest and icon
  integrity, service-worker precache completeness, citation presence, and an
  offline guard proving the app references **no external asset of any kind**.
- **Live, installable PWA** at
  <https://dimitres-kisimov.github.io/quantum-explainer/> — works fully
  offline after the first visit; no frameworks, no build step, no analytics.
- CI runs the same gates on every push (syntax check, both harnesses, ruff on
  the Python helper scripts, and an independent offline-guard grep).

## What I am NOT claiming

- **Not a quantum advantage demo.** A classical simulator of 1–2 qubits shows
  the *rules*, not a speedup — the app says this itself.
- **Two qubits is the ceiling**, deliberately: it is the largest system whose
  full state (4 complex amplitudes) can be shown honestly on screen.
- **Gates are ideal and noiseless.** Real hardware decoheres; the lessons say
  so, but the playground does not model noise.
- No "quantum will change everything" framing anywhere — where quantum
  computers may genuinely help is stated with the correct asymptotics and
  citations, nothing more.

## Deliverable

The **live PWA** (and this repo, runnable from a stock browser + Node), the
verification suite anyone can re-run in seconds, and a one-page PDF summary
(`deliverables/quantum_explainer_onepager.pdf`, regenerated by
`python tools/make_onepager.py`).
